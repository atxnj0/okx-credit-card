# OKX Credit Card: Is It a Credit Card, What Does It Cost, and Which Repayment Track Should You Pick? (Fees, Cashback Tiers and Limits Explained)

The OKX Card is not a credit card in the revolving sense. It is a Mastercard-branded secured charge card issued by WebBank, a Utah state-chartered bank, and the full statement balance is due every billing cycle. Nothing carries forward, no APR applies, and no interest accrues. It also charges no annual fee, no foreign transaction fee and no late fee, and applying triggers no credit check of any kind, hard or soft.

The spending power comes from stablecoins. You hold USDC, USDT or USDG in your OKX Pay wallet, authorize those coins for card use, and OKX places a hold on them equal to each purchase. They stay in your account as collateral until the balance is settled, either automatically one business day after each transaction or in dollars at the end of a 28-day cycle.

## Your spending limit is your wallet balance, recalculated on every purchase

Available Credit at the moment of authorization equals the lesser of two numbers: the value of unencumbered supported stablecoins in your Pay Wallet, or your Maximum Credit Limit minus the outstanding balance, including transactions that are authorized but not yet settled.

That structure produces a few behaviors worth knowing before you tap the card:

- **The limit moves in real time.** It rises and falls as you deposit or withdraw stablecoins. Moving unencumbered coins out of the Pay Wallet reduces your Available Credit by the same amount.
- **A Maximum Credit Limit sits on top.** WebBank can set or adjust it at any time at its discretion, and it may be lower than the dollar value of your stablecoin balance.
- **Valuation uses OKX's prevailing rate at authorization.** A stablecoin that trades below $1 converts to fewer dollars of credit, which is why the agreement warns that the USD equivalent of your wallet balance may differ from its nominal value.
- **Pre-authorization holds bite.** Hotels, car rentals and fuel stations can authorize more than the final bill. The excess stays encumbered and your Available Credit runs low for several days until settlement releases it.
- **The bank can decline anything.** Insufficient credit, a per-transaction or daily limit, a suspended account, a prohibited use, or a fraud flag are all listed grounds for refusal. There is no over-limit fee because over-limit transactions simply do not go through.

The collateral arrangement is narrow by design. WebBank takes a first-priority security interest in the stablecoins that are encumbered for card transactions, plus proceeds, and the agreement states that this interest does not extend to unencumbered assets in your Pay Wallet. It survives bankruptcy proceedings. You also waive the right to require the bank to pursue other collateral first, and the right to prior notice before liquidation on a default.

One structural point that gets missed: the Pay Wallet is a non-custodial wallet provided by OKX, not by the bank. The agreement puts responsibility for any Pay Wallet issue on OKX alone.

## Auto Repay vs Pay with Fiat: the choice that changes what happens to your coins

You pick a repayment track at account opening and can change it later in the OKX app. The two tracks produce very different outcomes when something goes wrong.

|  | Auto Repay | Pay with Fiat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When you pay | One business day after each transaction settles | Within 21 days of receiving the 28-day statement |
| How it is paid | OKX liquidates the encumbered stablecoins automatically | You send USD by fiat transfer or another accepted method |
| If you miss the deadline | Not applicable, liquidation already happened | Forced liquidation of encumbered stablecoins |
| Interest | None | None |
| Fits | Hands-off spending; OKX ties cashback to Autopay | Keeping stablecoins while paying from a dollar balance |

Under Auto Repay, liquidation happens at whatever market rate OKX applies at that moment, and OKX does not guarantee a specific rate. If the proceeds fall short of the balance, the shortfall is yours personally. Under Pay with Fiat, the encumbrance is released only once the entire outstanding balance is paid; miss the deadline and the forced liquidation happens anyway, and neither OKX nor the bank is liable for losses from selling at a depressed stablecoin price.

Switching rules are asymmetric. Moving from Auto Repay to Pay with Fiat takes effect once processed, and no later than the start of the next billing cycle. Moving the other way takes effect at the start of the next billing cycle.

The decision rule is short. If you want the card to run itself and you want the cashback that OKX ties to Autopay, choose Auto Repay and accept that your coins get sold one business day after every purchase. If your goal is to keep holding the stablecoins and settle in dollars, choose Pay with Fiat, and treat the 21-day window as a hard deadline rather than a suggestion, because the fallback is the same liquidation you were trying to avoid.

## Seven rules that decide whether this card works for you

1. **You cannot buy crypto with it.** Purchasing cryptocurrency or digital assets with the card is a prohibited use under the agreement.
2. **Business spending is prohibited.** Commercial or business use is listed as a default, alongside illegal purposes, sanctioned parties and jurisdictions, ATM withdrawals, cash advances, and payments on any credit account.
3. **There is no cash exit.** No ATM withdrawals, no cash advances, no cash equivalents. The card is a spending tool at merchants that accept Mastercard.
4. **Your stablecoins are not deposits.** Balances in the Pay Wallet are not FDIC-insured or covered by any deposit protection scheme.
5. **A depeg is your problem, financially.** If the encumbered stablecoins are worth less than the outstanding balance when liquidation happens, you remain personally liable for the full amount owed.
6. **Liquidation can be a taxable event.** Selling stablecoins to satisfy a card balance may count as disposing of a capital asset and may create US federal and state tax obligations, including capital gains tax. Neither OKX nor the bank provides tax advice.
7. **The account can be closed at will.** The agreement allows the bank to suspend, restrict or close the account with or without cause and with or without prior notice, including if the card program is discontinued.

None of these are hidden in fine print by industry standards, but each one changes the answer to "can this replace my daily card." For everyday spending against stablecoins you were holding anyway, the rules are workable. For business expenses or anything that needs cash access, they are disqualifying.

## FAQ

**Is there a physical OKX Card?**
No. The card is virtual-only at launch and works through Apple Pay and Google Pay for in-store contactless payments and online checkout.

**Does applying affect my credit score?**
No. OKX states there is no credit check and no credit score requirement, and that no inquiry of any kind, hard or soft, is performed during the application. Newly opened accounts are not reported to Experian, Equifax or TransUnion, and closing an unused card immediately is not reported either.

**Are there annual or foreign transaction fees?**
Neither. Both are listed as None in the agreement's fee disclosure, along with purchase fees, late payment fees and returned payment fees.

**How do I earn the cashback?**
OKX's launch announcement ties cashback to Autopay and eligible USDG purchases, starting at 2% and capped at $18,000 in annual rewards. The tier table on OKX's card pages shows rates from 2% to 5% across four tiers plus an OKX Pay APY of 4% to 10%. Check the rate shown on your own account, since the tier qualification figures are not explained publicly.

**What happens if USDC or USDT loses its peg while I owe money?**
You still owe the full balance in USD. If liquidation proceeds fall short, you are personally liable for the difference, and neither OKX nor WebBank is liable for losses from liquidating at a depressed value.

**What if a purchase exceeds my Available Credit?**
It is declined. There is no over-limit fee because the transaction does not clear.
